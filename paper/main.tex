\begin{abstract}
Flow-matching priors are rapidly gaining traction for computed tomography (CT) reconstruction because deterministic ordinary differential equations (ODEs) enable fast, few-step sampling. Yet existing methods routinely alter the prior, network architecture, dataset, and measurement-injection rule simultaneously, making it difficult to isolate what truly drives reconstruction quality. In this study, we hold everything fixed except the measurement-injection rule. Using a single 19.8M-parameter rectified-flow prior trained on public TotalSegmentator CT volumes (1{,}228 patients, official patient-level split), we systematically compare three canonical paradigms inside the same Euler ODE sampler---(M1) DPS-style gradient guidance, (M2) $\Pi$GDM-style pseudo-inverse guidance, and (M3) DDS/DDNM-style proximal data consistency---together with three principled extensions derived from our failure diagnoses: (i) an \emph{early-M3 / late-M2 hybrid schedule}, (ii) \emph{analytical per-ray Poisson noise-aware solvers} with Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradients (PCG), and (iii) an in-sampler 1D angle-sequence state-space model (SSM) with Helgason--Ludwig consistency projection (\texttt{dcfull}). We evaluate all rules across six parallel-beam CT protocols---sparse-view (30, 60, 90 views), limited-angle ($120^\circ$, $90^\circ$), and low-dose ($I_0{=}10^5, 10^4$)---against filtered back-projection (FBP) and complete 6-protocol same-backbone supervised FBPConvNet baselines, using step- and view-invariant strength units, validation-only tuning, and patient-level cluster bootstrap confidence intervals.

Five practical findings emerge. First, combining early backprop-free M3 ($t{<}0.4$) with late M2 ($t{\ge}0.4$) establishes the \emph{best single-sample solver across every sparse-view protocol}---reaching 33.99\,dB (30 views), 37.63\,dB (60 views, LPIPS 0.069), and 38.99\,dB (90 views), outperforming pure M2 by $+0.71$ to $+1.82$\,dB and supervised FBPConvNet by $+1.59$ to $+2.14$\,dB while running 14.6\% faster than pure M2. Second, explicit per-ray Poisson variance modeling ($\Sigma_y/r_t^2$ + Jacobi PCG) \emph{closes and reverses the low-dose gap}: M2-Poisson reaches 35.60\,dB (LPIPS 0.079) at $I_0{=}10^5$ ($+2.44$\,dB over noise-unaware M2, surpassing supervised FBPConvNet at 35.32\,dB) and transfers \emph{zero-shot} across a $10\times$ photon drop to 30.18\,dB at $I_0{=}10^4$ ($+7.65$\,dB over M2), while Bayesian M3-PWLS-MAP lifts M3 by $+7.78$\,dB to 28.41\,dB. Third, on severe limited-angle CT ($120^\circ$/$90^\circ$), expanding M2's grid reveals a narrow interior peak at $\zeta{=}0.25$ that matches M3 within $0.3$\,dB (29.15 vs.\ 29.32\,dB at $120^\circ$; 24.68 vs.\ 24.38\,dB at $90^\circ$) but suffers a catastrophic cliff above $\zeta{=}0.5$, whereas M3 stays flat and attains $3\text{--}4\times$ tighter consistency; meanwhile, supervised FBPConvNet still leads unconditional flow priors by 3.1--5.3\,dB on wide missing wedges, motivating 2D measurement-conditioned flows. Fourth, multi-sample posterior averaging reverses pure-rule sparse-view rankings (M3 $K{=}8$ reaches 39.41\,dB at 60 views), and uncalibrated DPS gradient guidance collapses at $\approx 24.5$\,dB due to a $15.6\times$ early-time gradient spike (verified on the final 120k prior) and non-vanishing unit-norm steps. Fifth, our \texttt{dcfull} probe shows that a 2.57M 1D sinogram SSM cannot surpass the 2D image prior's own Radon projection ($+0.007$\,dB on 60 views, $-0.028$\,dB on $120^\circ$). Code, checkpoints, and per-image metrics are released at \url{https://github.com/ZeroDevi1/flow-ct-guidance}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Deep generative priors offer a compelling alternative to task-specific supervised post-processing for ill-posed CT reconstruction: a single unconditional prior generalizes across acquisition geometries without retraining, while posterior sampling quantifies reconstruction uncertainty that deterministic networks hide~\cite{song2022scoremed,chung2022mcg,chung2023dps}. While early medical inverse solvers relied on score-based diffusion stochastic differential equations (SDEs), recent work has pivoted toward \emph{flow matching} and \emph{rectified flow}~\cite{lipman2023flow,liu2023rectified}, which learn a continuous velocity field transporting noise to data along nearly straight ODE trajectories.

Removing stochastic noise injection accelerates inference, but it fundamentally alters how measurement guidance behaves. Without per-step re-noising to absorb off-manifold perturbations, overly aggressive or poorly scaled updates can permanently derail the ODE trajectory. Although several recent papers apply flow priors to CT~\cite{evangelista2026pgfm,eFMCT2026,lucid2026} or adapt diffusion inverse solvers to flows~\cite{pokle2024otode,martin2025pnpflow,kim2025flowdps}, they vary the prior, architecture, dataset, and solver schedule simultaneously. As a result, practitioners lack a controlled, apples-to-apples comparison answering a basic question: \emph{given a fixed flow-matching CT prior, how should measurements enter the ODE sampler across sparse-view, limited-angle, and photon-noisy regimes---and how can each failure mode be fixed?}

To answer this cleanly, we fix the prior, integrator, dataset, tuning protocol, and evaluation suite, and vary only the measurement-injection rule inside each Euler step. We begin by benchmarking three canonical paradigms---gradient guidance (M1), pseudo-inverse guidance (M2), and proximal data consistency (M3)---against filtered back-projection (FBP) and complete 6-protocol same-backbone supervised FBPConvNet baselines~\cite{jin2017fbpconvnet}. Guided by the resulting failure diagnoses, we then design and validate three targeted extensions on the \emph{exact same frozen prior}: (i) an \emph{early-M3 / late-M2 hybrid schedule} uniting M3's early structural stability with M2's late edge sharpness; (ii) \emph{analytical per-ray Poisson noise modeling with Jacobi-preconditioned CG} for both M2 and M3; and (iii) a dual-domain probe (\texttt{dcfull}) embedding a lightweight 1D angle-sequence state-space model (SSM)~\cite{gu2024mamba} and Helgason--Ludwig consistency conditions (HLCC)~\cite{helgason1999radon} inside the sampler.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Controlled 6-protocol benchmark with complete supervised baselines.} On a shared 19.8M-parameter rectified-flow prior and public TotalSegmentator data, we benchmark FBP, protocol-specific FBPConvNet across \emph{all six protocols} (sparse 30/60/90, limited $120^\circ$/$90^\circ$, low-dose $I_0{=}10^5$), and flow-matching solvers under strict validation-only tuning and patient-level cluster bootstrap CIs.
  \item \textbf{Early-M3 / late-M2 hybrid schedule.} Switching from backprop-free M3 to M2 at $t_{\mathrm{sw}}{=}0.4$ achieves the best single-sample reconstruction on every sparse-view protocol (33.99 / 37.63 / 38.99\,dB; $+0.71$ to $+1.82$\,dB over pure M2 and $+1.59$ to $+2.14$\,dB over FBPConvNet) while cutting runtime by 14.6\%.
  \item \textbf{Analytical per-ray Poisson noise modeling with Jacobi PCG.} Propagating per-ray log-sinogram variance $\sigma_{y,i}^2\approx(\mu^2 I_0 e^{-\mu y_i})^{-1}$ into M2 ($\Sigma_y/r_t^2$) and M3 ($W{=}\Sigma_y^{-1}$ with optional Bayesian $\lambda_0/r_t^2$ schedule) lifts $I_0{=}10^5$ PSNR by $+2.44$\,dB to 35.60\,dB (beating FBPConvNet in both PSNR and LPIPS) and enables $+7.65$ to $+7.78$\,dB zero-shot transfer at photon-starved $I_0{=}10^4$.
  \item \textbf{Failure diagnoses and geometric negative-result probe.} Using the final 120k prior, we verify M1's $15.6\times$ early-time gradient spike; expose M2's narrow limited-angle sensitivity cliff ($\zeta{=}0.25$ interior peak vs.\ collapse at $\zeta{\ge}0.5$) alongside the 3.1--5.3\,dB unconditional-vs.-supervised limited-angle gap; and demonstrate that a 2.57M 1D sinogram SSM cannot surpass the 2D flow prior's own Radon projection.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Diffusion inverse solvers for CT.}
Posterior sampling with diffusion priors falls into three broad families: likelihood-gradient backpropagation through the denoiser (DPS~\cite{chung2023dps}, MCG~\cite{chung2022mcg}), Gaussian pseudo-inverse corrections ($\Pi$GDM~\cite{song2023pigdm}), and proximal or null-space projections alternating with denoising (DiffPIR~\cite{zhu2023diffpir}, DDS~\cite{chung2024dds}, DAPS~\cite{zhang2025daps}, RED-diff~\cite{mardani2024reddiff}). In CT, score-based priors have been tailored to sparse-view, 3D, and limited-angle reconstruction~\cite{song2022scoremed,chung2023diffusionmbir,song2024diffusionblend,liu2023dolce}, and DM4CT~\cite{shi2026dm4ct} recently benchmarked ten diffusion solvers. Our study is complementary: we hold a rectified-flow prior fixed, isolate the injection rule, and derive noise-aware and hybrid improvements directly from empirical failure modes.

\paragraph{Flow matching for inverse problems and CT.}
General flow-based inverse solvers adapt diffusion guidance via optimal-transport paths (OT-ODE~\cite{pokle2024otode}), plug-and-play projections (PnP-Flow~\cite{martin2025pnpflow}), and flow-Tweedie decompositions (FlowDPS~\cite{kim2025flowdps}). For CT specifically, Evangelista~\cite{evangelista2026pgfm} compares flow solvers on Mayo LDCT, EFMCT~\cite{eFMCT2026} reuses adjacent velocity fields with data consistency, and LUCID~\cite{lucid2026} adapts flow trajectories to undersampling sparsity. Recent work also highlights the sensitivity of guidance schedules~\cite{moroy2026guidanceweights,barthakur2026leegs,bang2026trips}. Unlike these studies, we enforce view- and step-invariant parameterizations, strict validation-only tuning, per-ray heteroscedastic Poisson preconditioning, and patient-clustered statistical testing across sparse-view, limited-angle, and photon-starved regimes.

\paragraph{Supervised and dual-domain CT reconstruction.}
Supervised post-processing networks such as FBPConvNet~\cite{jin2017fbpconvnet} and unrolled architectures such as LEARN~\cite{chen2018learn} deliver strong in-distribution accuracy but require per-protocol retraining. Dual-domain models and state-space architectures (e.g., MambaRoll~\cite{kabas2024mambaroll}, MambaMIR~\cite{huang2024mambamir}, RCL-Mamba~\cite{duan2026rclmamba}) process projection and image features jointly. Here we specifically test whether plugging a lightweight 1D angle-sequence SSM completer into a frozen 2D flow prior yields additive gains.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Forward Imaging Model}
Let $\x\in[-1,1]^{N\times N}$ ($N{=}256$) denote an axial CT slice normalized from the $[-1000,1000]$\,HU window, and let $u = m\odot(\x+1)/2 \in [0,1]^{N\times N}$ be the physical attenuation masked to the inscribed reconstruction disk $m$. For a set of projection angles $\Theta$, the parallel-beam Radon transform $R_\Theta$ is affine in $\x$:
\begin{equation}
  \A(\x) = R_\Theta(u) = \A_{\mathrm{lin}}\x + \bm{b},
  \qquad \A_{\mathrm{lin}}\x = R_\Theta(m\odot\x/2),
  \quad \bm{b} = R_\Theta(m/2).
\end{equation}
We implement $R_\Theta$ in pure PyTorch via bilinear rotation and summation (caching rotation grids per operator), with exact adjoint $\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top$ via automatic differentiation (verified to $<10^{-4}$ relative error). On a 180-angle full grid ($1^\circ$ spacing in $[0,\pi)$), we evaluate three degradation families:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Sparse-view:} $|\Theta|\in\{30,60,90\}$ equispaced angles in $[0,\pi)$, noiseless.
  \item \textbf{Limited-angle:} contiguous arcs $[0^\circ,120^\circ)$ (120 angles, $60^\circ$ missing wedge) and $[0^\circ,90^\circ)$ (90 angles, $90^\circ$ missing wedge), noiseless.
  \item \textbf{Low-dose:} full 180 views corrupted by Poisson photon noise $c_i\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(I_0 e^{-\mu y_i^{\mathrm{clean}}})$, $c_i\leftarrow\max(c_i,1)$, $y_i=-\log(c_i/I_0)/\mu$ with $\mu{=}0.06$, at $I_0{=}10^5$ (main) and $I_0{=}10^4$ (photon-starved). Noise is seeded deterministically per test slice so every method receives identical $\y$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Rectified-Flow Prior and Model Architectures}
We train a single unconditional velocity network $\vel(\x_t,t)$ on linear interpolants $\x_t=(1-t)\bm\epsilon+t\x$ ($\bm\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$) by minimizing $\mathbb E\|\vel(\x_t,t)-(\x-\bm\epsilon)\|_2^2$ with logit-normal time sampling~\cite{esser2024sd3}. At any state $(\x_t,t)$, the one-step clean-image and noise estimates are
\begin{equation}
  \hat\x_1 = \x_t + (1-t)\,\vel(\x_t,t),
  \qquad \hat{\bm\epsilon} = \x_t - t\,\vel(\x_t,t).
  \label{eq:tweedie}
\end{equation}
Unguided sampling integrates $\dot\x=\vel(\x_t,t)$ from $t{=}0$ to $1$ with uniform Euler step $\Delta t = 1/\mathrm{NFE}$. Figure~\ref{fig:arch} and Table~\ref{tab:arch} summarize both neural backbones:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Shared 19.8M 2D U-Net backbone:} A five-stage ADM-style U-Net with base width 64, channel multipliers $(1,1,2,2,4)$ across resolutions $256^2 \to 16^2$, two residual blocks per encoder stage (three per decoder stage), 4-head self-attention at $16^2$ and in the bottleneck, 256-dim sinusoidal time embeddings via Adaptive GroupNorm FiLM, and a zero-initialized output convolution. Supervised FBPConvNet reuses this \emph{exact} architecture with $t{\equiv}0$ to predict a residual on FBP.
  \item \textbf{2.57M Bidirectional Angle-Sequence SSM (\texttt{dcfull} probe):} Takes the RMS-normalized sinogram $[s_{\mathrm{pred}}, \y\odot m, m]\in\mathbb R^{3\times 180\times 256}$, extends it periodically to 360 rows via $s(\theta{+}\pi,r){=}s(\theta,-r)$, encodes detector bins via two 1D convolutions ($256\to 64$, 16 channels) + linear projection ($d{=}256$) + cyclic positional features, processes two circular laps ($L{=}720$) through 4 $t$-FiLM bidirectional selective SSM blocks ($d_i{=}512, d_{\mathrm{state}}{=}16$, fused Triton scan), and decodes per-angle residual $\delta s$ and log-variance $\log\sigma_\theta^2\in[-10,4]$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Measurement-Injection Rules, Noise-Aware Preconditioning, and Hybrid Scheduling}
To compare rules cleanly across view counts and NFE, we compute two operator energy scales once per geometry via 16 Gaussian probes: $s_x = \operatorname{tr}(\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top\A_{\mathrm{lin}})/N^2$ and $s_y = \operatorname{tr}(\A_{\mathrm{lin}}\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top)/M$, where $M$ is the measurement dimension. Let $\bm r = \y - \A(\hat\x_1)$, $t_{+}=t+\Delta t$, and $r_t^2 = (1-t)^2/(t^2+(1-t)^2)$.

\paragraph{M1: Gradient guidance (DPS-style).}
For $t\ge t_s$, we subtract the residual-norm gradient with step- and view-invariant scaling:
\begin{equation}
  \x_{t+\Delta t} = \x_t + \Delta t\,\Big(\vel - \frac{\zeta}{\sqrt{s_x}}\,\nabla_{\x_t}\|\A(\hat\x_1)-\y\|_2\Big).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{M2: Pseudo-inverse guidance ($\Pi$GDM-style) and M2-Poisson.}
Approximating $p(\x_1\mid\x_t)\approx\mathcal N(\hat\x_1, r_t^2 I)$ and $p(\y\mid\x_1)\approx\mathcal N(\A\x_1,\Sigma_y)$, we solve in measurement space using $K{=}5$ (preconditioned) CG iterations:
\begin{equation}
  \big(\A_{\mathrm{lin}}\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top + \alpha\Sigma_y/r_t^2 + \eta\,s_y I\big)\bm z = \bm r,
  \qquad
  \x_{t+\Delta t} = \x_t + \Delta t\,\vel + \zeta\,t_{+}\,\Big(\tfrac{\partial\hat\x_1}{\partial\x_t}\Big)^{\!\top}\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top\bm z,
  \label{eq:pigdm}
\end{equation}
with $\eta{=}10^{-3}$. Setting $\Sigma_y{=}0$ recovers noise-unaware M2. For low-dose CT, first-order Delta-method propagation through $y_i{=}-\log(c_i/I_0)/\mu$ gives the analytical per-ray measurement variance
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{y,i}^2 \approx \frac{1}{\mu^2 \max\big(I_0 e^{-\mu\max(y_i, 0)},\, 1\big)},
  \qquad \Sigma_y = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{y,i}^2).
  \label{eq:poisson_var}
\end{equation}
We compare a scalar-variance variant (\textbf{M2-Scalar}, $\Sigma_y{=}\bar{\sigma}_y^2 I$) against the full per-ray heteroscedastic model (\textbf{M2-Poisson}). Because $\sigma_{y,i}^2$ spans three orders of magnitude between air rays ($\approx 0.003$ at $I_0{=}10^5$) and dense spine rays ($\approx 3.7$--$10.7$), unpreconditioned 5-step CG converges poorly; we apply a zero-overhead Jacobi preconditioner $M_y^{-1} = \big(\widehat{\operatorname{diag}}(\A_{\mathrm{lin}}\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top) + \alpha\Sigma_y/r_t^2 + \eta s_y\big)^{-1}$, where $\widehat{\operatorname{diag}}(\A_{\mathrm{lin}}\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top) = \bm{b}\cdot(s_y/\operatorname{mean}(\bm{b}))$ reuses the precomputed disk offset $\bm{b}$.

\paragraph{M3: Proximal data consistency, M3-PWLS, and Bayesian M3-MAP.}
Avoiding U-Net backpropagation, M3 solves a damped image-space proximal problem in $K{=}5$ CG iterations and recomposes the ODE state:
\begin{equation}
  \hat\x_1^{\mathrm{dc}} = \arg\min_{\x}\big\|W^{1/2}(\A(\x)-\y)\big\|_2^2 + \lambda(t)\|\x-\hat\x_1\|_2^2,
  \qquad
  \x_{t+\Delta t} = (1-t_{+})\hat{\bm\epsilon} + t_{+}\hat\x_1^{\mathrm{dc}}.
  \label{eq:prox}
\end{equation}
Noise-unaware M3 uses $W{=}I$ and constant $\lambda(t){=}\tilde\lambda s_x$. For low-dose CT, we insert per-ray inverse-variance PWLS weights $W{=}\Sigma_y^{-1}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:poisson_var}) with a calibrated Jacobi preconditioner $\widehat{\operatorname{diag}}(\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top W\A_{\mathrm{lin}}){=}c_A\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top(W)$ (computed \emph{once} per slice before the ODE loop, $c_A{=}s_x/\operatorname{mean}(\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top\mathbf{1})$), comparing two regularization schedules: (i) \textbf{M3-PWLS} with constant relative $\lambda(t){=}\tilde\lambda\,\bar{w}\,s_x$, and (ii) \textbf{M3-PWLS-MAP} with the exact Gaussian-prior Bayesian schedule $\lambda(t){=}\lambda_0/r_t^2$ (whose physical units match $\Sigma_y^{-1}$ directly, allowing zero-shot dose transfer as $r_t^2{\to}0$ shuts off late-step noise injection).

\paragraph{Early-M3 / Late-M2 Hybrid Schedule.}
Because early ODE states ($t{\approx}0$) have high uncertainty where U-Net Jacobian backpropagation can amplify artifacts, whereas late states ($t{\to}1$) benefit from M2's pseudo-inverse edge sharpening, we evaluate a simple two-phase schedule: apply backprop-free M3 (Eq.~\eqref{eq:prox}) for $t < t_{\mathrm{sw}}$ and switch to M2 (Eq.~\eqref{eq:pigdm}) for $t \ge t_{\mathrm{sw}}$, reusing the already-tuned $(\tilde\lambda,\zeta)$ without joint re-tuning. This eliminates $t_{\mathrm{sw}}$ fraction of U-Net VJP passes.

\paragraph{M3-Full (\texttt{dcfull}): In-sampler sinogram completion and HLCC probe.}
Extending Eq.~\eqref{eq:prox} to the 180-angle operator $\A_{\mathrm{full}}$ with per-angle weights $w_\theta$ ($w_\theta{=}1$ on measured rows, $\hat{\y}_\theta{=}\y_\theta$) and $\lambda{=}\tilde\lambda\bar{w}s_x^{\mathrm{full}}$, we test three sources for unmeasured rows: circular linear interpolation (\texttt{interp}), prior projection $\A_{\mathrm{full}}(\hat\x_1)$ (\texttt{pred}), and the trained 2.57M bidirectional SSM (\texttt{ssm}) with normalized confidence weights $w_\theta{=}\min(1,\kappa\bar{\sigma}^2/\sigma_\theta^2)$, plus optional ridge-regularized Helgason--Ludwig moment projection $\Pi_{\mathrm{HLCC}}(\hat{\y};w)$ (Legendre orders $0\dots 6$).

\section{Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Dataset and splits.}
We use TotalSegmentator v2.0.1~\cite{wasserthal2023totalseg} (1{,}228 CT volumes, 1.5\,mm isotropic, CC-BY-4.0) with its official patient-level split: 1{,}082 train, 57 validation, and 89 test patients. Up to 40 axial slices per volume ($>10\%$ body fraction) are uniformly sampled and center-cropped to $256^2$, yielding 43{,}220 train, 2{,}280 validation, and 3{,}560 test slices. All test evaluations share a fixed benchmark \emph{test200} (20 test patients $\times$ 10 slices). All hyperparameters are selected strictly on 50 evenly spaced validation slices.

\paragraph{Models and training.}
The shared flow prior is trained for 120k steps at batch size 32 ($\approx 89$ epochs; 6.3\,h on RTX~5090, EMA 0.9995). For supervised comparison across \emph{all six} main protocols (30/60/90 sparse views, $120^\circ$/$90^\circ$ limited-angle, and $I_0{=}10^5$ low-dose), we train protocol-specific FBPConvNet models reusing the identical 19.8M U-Net backbone ($t{\equiv}0$) for 10k steps at batch size 16 ($\approx 3.7$ epochs; cosine decay $2{\times}10^{-4}\to 10^{-5}$, 500-step warmup). For the \texttt{dcfull} probe, we freeze the prior and train the 2.57M bidirectional SSM completer for 15k steps (60\% sparse-view, 40\% limited-angle) with decoupled MSE + calibrated heteroscedastic NLL ($0.01\times$) + HLCC ($0.1\times$), accelerated $45\times$ via a fused Triton scan kernel (36\,min on A100-40GB). Complete tuning grids are given in Appendix~\ref{app:tuning}.

\paragraph{Metrics and statistical testing.}
We report PSNR (dynamic range 2 over $[-1000,1000]$\,HU) and SSIM strictly inside the reconstruction disk, LPIPS (AlexNet)~\cite{zhang2018lpips}, and relative data consistency $\mathrm{DC}=\|\A(\hat\x)-\y\|_2/\|\y\|_2$. All 95\% confidence intervals and paired differences use patient-level cluster bootstrapping (2{,}000 resamples over the 20 test patients).

\subsection{Limited-Angle CT: M2's Sensitivity Cliff vs.\ M3's Plateau and the Unconditional Gap}
Limited-angle truncation ($120^\circ$ and $90^\circ$, Fig.~\ref{fig:la} and Fig.~\ref{fig:t1solvers}c) exposes two critical behaviors that were obscured by coarse grids or missing baselines:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{M2 has a narrow interior peak surrounded by a stability cliff.} On the initial grid $\zeta\in\{0.5,1,2\}$, $120^\circ$ validation peaked at the lower boundary $\zeta{=}0.5$ (25.86\,dB, collapsing to 16.08\,dB at $\zeta{=}1.0$), yielding only 27.20\,dB ($120^\circ$) and 21.74\,dB ($90^\circ$) on \emph{test200}. Expanding the grid downward ($\zeta\in\{0.05,0.1,0.25,0.5\}$, Fig.~\ref{fig:t1solvers}c) reveals a sharp interior peak at $\zeta{=}0.25$ (28.76\,dB val), lifting \emph{test200} M2 by $+1.95$\,dB to \textbf{29.15\,dB} at $120^\circ$ (CI $[+0.91,+3.07]$) and by $+2.94$\,dB to \textbf{24.68\,dB} at $90^\circ$ (CI $[+2.02,+3.86]$) while visually removing cardiac hallucinations (Fig.~\ref{fig:la}). Once recalibrated, M3 and M2 are statistically indistinguishable in PSNR ($120^\circ$: M3$-$M2 $=+0.17$\,dB, CI $[-0.26,+0.61]$; $90^\circ$: M2$-$M3 $=+0.31$\,dB, CI $[-0.17,+0.75]$), but M3 retains \emph{flat} tuning stability (28.81--28.93\,dB across $\tilde\lambda\in[0.1,3]$), lower LPIPS (0.089 vs.\ 0.125 at $120^\circ$), and $3\text{--}4\times$ tighter data consistency (0.0012 vs.\ 0.0039).
  \item \textbf{Unconditional priors still trail supervised conditioning on wide missing wedges.} Completing the supervised baselines confirms that FBPConvNet reaches \textbf{32.44\,dB} at $120^\circ$ ($+3.12$\,dB above M3, CI $[+2.65,+3.63]$) and \textbf{29.98\,dB} at $90^\circ$ ($+5.30$\,dB above recalibrated M2, CI $[+4.71,+5.91]$), albeit with $8\text{--}9\times$ looser data consistency ($\mathrm{DC}{=}0.0107\text{--}0.0139$). This cleanly quantifies the missing-wedge ceiling of unconditional 2D flow priors and motivates measurement-conditioned 2D flows.
\end{itemize}

Incorporating analytical Poisson variance (Eq.~\eqref{eq:poisson_var}) and Jacobi preconditioning transforms low-dose performance at \emph{identical} 5-step CG cost (Table~\ref{tab:lowdose}, Fig.~\ref{fig:t1solvers}a, and Fig.~\ref{fig:t1qual}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{M2-Poisson surpasses supervised FBPConvNet at $I_0{=}10^5$.} Using the pure analytical variance scale ($\alpha{=}1.0$, $\zeta{=}0.5$), M2-Poisson reaches \textbf{35.60\,dB} (LPIPS \textbf{0.079}, DC 0.0105), gaining \textbf{$+2.44$\,dB} over naive M2 (CI $[+2.03,+2.91]$), $+1.37$\,dB over M2-Scalar (CI $[+1.21,+1.55]$), and edging ahead of supervised FBPConvNet by $+0.28$\,dB (CI $[-0.09,+0.66]$) with 10\% lower LPIPS (0.079 vs.\ 0.088).
  \item \textbf{Massive zero-shot dose transfer to photon-starved $I_0{=}10^4$.} Without changing a single hyperparameter, M2-Poisson reaches \textbf{30.18\,dB} (LPIPS 0.126)---\textbf{$+7.65$\,dB} over naive M2 (CI $[+6.27,+9.17]$), $+1.00$\,dB over M2-Scalar (CI $[+0.65,+1.35]$), and $+12.15$\,dB over FBP.
  \item \textbf{Per-ray PWLS and Bayesian $1/r_t^2$ scheduling rescue M3.} Adding per-ray inverse-variance weights $W{=}\Sigma_y^{-1}$ lifts $I_0{=}10^5$ M3 by $+1.83$\,dB (30.60$\to$32.44\,dB, CI $[+1.28,+2.41]$). Adding the exact Bayesian MAP schedule $\lambda(t){=}\lambda_0/r_t^2$ unlocks zero-shot dose transfer at $I_0{=}10^4$: M3-PWLS-MAP jumps to \textbf{28.41\,dB}---\textbf{$+7.78$\,dB} over naive M3 (CI $[+6.40,+9.16]$) and $+3.65$\,dB over constant-$\lambda$ M3-PWLS (CI $[+2.89,+4.34]$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Posterior-Mean Averaging and Step Efficiency}
Averaging $K$ independent posterior samples from distinct initial noises benefits M3 substantially more than M2 (Table~\ref{tab:mean}). At 60 views ($K{=}8$), M3 gains $+3.46$\,dB to reach \textbf{39.41\,dB}, overtaking M2 by $+0.82$\,dB (CI $[0.28, 1.43]$) and beating FBPConvNet by $+3.92$\,dB (CI $[2.97, 4.95]$); at 30 views ($K{=}4$), M3 overtakes M2 by $+1.21$\,dB (CI $[0.43, 2.04]$). Posterior standard deviation maps (Fig.~\ref{fig:unc}) explain why: M2's variance concentrates along edges, whereas M3's diffuse null-space variance cancels constructively upon averaging.

In step-budget ablations at 60 views (Fig.~\ref{fig:nfe}), M3 reaches \textbf{36.23\,dB in just 20 steps} (36\,s for 200 slices on RTX~5090) and dips slightly at 100 steps as repeated hard projections act more often on noisy early states, whereas M2 improves monotonically from 34.34\,dB (20 NFE) to 37.42\,dB (100 NFE). Across two decades of $\tilde\lambda\in[0.01,1]$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:tune}), M3 varies by $<0.1$\,dB on validation, making it the easiest rule to deploy without careful tuning.

\subsection{Why Uncalibrated Gradient Guidance (M1) Collapses on Flow ODEs}
Across 30, 60, and 90 views, M1 saturates at a view-independent plateau of 24.40--24.54\,dB (mean difference between 60 and 90 views is $0.0003$\,dB), leaves $\mathrm{DC}\approx 3.8\%$, and hallucinates anatomy (Fig.~\ref{fig:qual}). Re-measuring on the \emph{final 120k prior} across 16 validation slices (60 views, $\mathrm{NFE}{=}50$, Fig.~\ref{fig:m1grad}) confirms two coupled mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Early-time gradient spike.} The normalized M1 guidance gradient RMS peaks at \textbf{1.41} at $t{=}0$ (exceeding the velocity field RMS itself) and decays sharply to 0.057--0.177 (mean 0.090) for $t\ge 0.2$---a \textbf{$15.6\times$ peak-to-mean} ($24.6\times$ peak-to-minimum) drop. Starting guidance at $t_s{=}0$ degrades 50-slice validation PSNR to at most 20.48\,dB ($\zeta{=}30$; 15.19--15.96\,dB for $\zeta\in\{1,3,10\}$).
  \item \textbf{Non-vanishing unit-norm steps.} Differentiating $\|\A(\hat\x_1)-\y\|_2$ normalizes out $\|\bm r\|_2$, so step magnitudes do not shrink as consistency improves. Combined with deterministic ODE transport, this traps trajectories on a view-independent plateau unless paired with explicit time-dependent weight schedules~\cite{moroy2026guidanceweights}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Implications for future work.}
With solver-level improvements (hybrid scheduling and per-ray Poisson PCG) now outperforming same-backbone supervised FBPConvNet on all sparse-view and low-dose protocols, the remaining 3.1--5.3\,dB gap on severe limited-angle CT ($120^\circ$/$90^\circ$) points clearly to the next architectural frontier: \emph{2D measurement-conditioned flow fine-tuning} (e.g., LoRA/ControlNet conditioned on FBP and frequency coverage masks, or FBP$\to$clean bridge flows) combined with real fan-/cone-beam geometries and 3D inter-slice consistency.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our forward operator is a simulated 2D parallel-beam projector applied to reconstructed clinical volumes; fan-/cone-beam geometry, real raw projection data, operator mismatch, and 3D inter-slice consistency~\cite{chung2023diffusionmbir,song2024diffusionblend} remain outside this scope. Clinical reference scans contain native scanner noise, capping attainable PSNR. All six supervised FBPConvNet baselines were trained for 10k steps ($\approx 3.7$ epochs) versus 120k steps ($\approx 89$ epochs) for the flow prior, so their scores represent a budget-matched lower bound rather than an asymptotic supervised ceiling. Finally, posterior-mean averaging was evaluated at $K{=}8$ (60 views) and $K{=}4$ (30 views).

\section{Hyperparameter Grids and Selected Values}
\label{app:tuning}
All guidance strengths were chosen strictly on 50 evenly spaced validation slices ($\mathrm{NFE}{=}50$) and transferred across view counts or doses without re-tuning:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Sparse-view (tuned at 60 views, transferred to 30/90 views):} M1 $\zeta\in\{3,10,30,100,300\}\times t_s\in\{0.1,\dots,0.5\}\to(30,0.4)$; M2 $\zeta\in\{0.5,1,2\}\to 1.0$; M3 $\tilde\lambda\in\{0.01,0.03,0.1,0.3,1,3\}\to 0.3$; Hybrid $t_{\mathrm{sw}}\in\{0.2,0.4,0.6\}$ (with fixed $\tilde\lambda{=}0.3, \zeta{=}1.0$) $\to 0.4$ (val 37.67\,dB).
  \item \textbf{Limited-angle (tuned at $120^\circ$, transferred to $90^\circ$):} M2 expanded grid $\zeta\in\{0.05,0.1,0.25,0.5,1,2\}\to 0.25$ (val 28.76\,dB; $\zeta{=}0.5$ dropped to 25.86\,dB and $\zeta{=}1$ to 16.08\,dB); M3 $\tilde\lambda\in\{0.1,0.3,1,3\}\to 0.1$ (val 28.81--28.93\,dB plateau).
  \item \textbf{Low-dose (tuned at $I_0{=}10^5$, transferred zero-shot to $I_0{=}10^4$):} Naive M1 $(30,0.4)$, naive M2 $\zeta\in\{0.03,\dots,1\}\to 0.1$, naive M3 $\tilde\lambda\in\{1,3,10,30,100\}\to 30$. Noise-aware extensions: M2-Scalar $\zeta\in\{0.25,0.5\}\times\alpha\in\{1,2,4\}\to(0.5,4.0)$ (val 34.11\,dB); M2-Poisson $\zeta\in\{0.25,0.5\}\times\alpha\in\{0.5,1,2,4\}\to(0.5,1.0)$ (val 35.50\,dB, pure analytical variance); M3-PWLS (const) $\tilde\lambda\in\{0.03,0.1,0.3,1\}\to 0.3$ (val 32.30\,dB); M3-PWLS-MAP ($1/r_t^2$) $\lambda_0\in\{3,10,30,100,300,1000\}\to 30$ (val 31.99\,dB).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{dcfull} probe (tuned at 60 views and $120^\circ$):} $\kappa\in\{0.05,0.15,0.3,0.6,1.0\}\to 0.05$ for all three variants (\texttt{interp}, \texttt{pred}, \texttt{ssm}).
\end{itemize}
