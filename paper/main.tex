\begin{abstract}
Flow-matching priors are rapidly gaining traction for computed tomography (CT) reconstruction because deterministic ordinary differential equations (ODEs) enable fast, few-step sampling. Yet existing methods routinely alter the prior, network architecture, dataset, and measurement-injection rule simultaneously, making it difficult to isolate what truly drives reconstruction quality. In this study, we hold everything fixed except the measurement-injection rule. Using a single 19.8M-parameter rectified-flow prior trained on public TotalSegmentator CT volumes (1{,}228 patients, official patient-level split), we systematically compare three canonical injection mechanisms inside the same Euler ODE sampler: (M1) DPS-style gradient guidance, (M2) $\Pi$GDM-style pseudo-inverse guidance, and (M3) DDS/DDNM-style proximal data consistency. We evaluate all rules across six parallel-beam CT protocols---sparse-view (30, 60, 90 views), limited-angle ($120^\circ$, $90^\circ$), and low-dose ($I_0{=}10^5, 10^4$)---using step- and view-invariant strength units, validation-only hyperparameter selection, and patient-level cluster bootstrap confidence intervals. We further probe whether augmenting M3 with an in-sampler, 1D angle-sequence state-space model (SSM) for full-angle sinogram completion and Helgason--Ludwig consistency projection improves performance.

Four practical findings emerge. First, the optimal single-sample rule is \emph{regime-dependent}: M2 leads on moderate sparse-view CT (36.93\,dB at 60 views, $+1.43$\,dB over a same-backbone supervised FBPConvNet), whereas M3 decisively outperforms M2 on severely ill-posed limited-angle CT ($+2.12$\,dB at $120^\circ$ and $+2.63$\,dB at $90^\circ$) and achieves the best step efficiency (36.23\,dB at 20 NFE). Second, averaging posterior samples reverses the sparse-view ranking: M3 gains $+3.46$\,dB with $K{=}8$ samples at 60 views to reach 39.41\,dB ($+3.92$\,dB over FBPConvNet). Third, uncalibrated DPS-style gradient guidance collapses on the flow ODE, plateauing at $\approx 24.5$\,dB regardless of view count due to a $\sim\!60\times$ early-time Jacobian spike and non-vanishing unit-norm steps. Fourth, under photon noise without an explicit noise model, supervised FBPConvNet remains ahead ($+2.17$\,dB at $I_0{=}10^5$), and our sinogram-completion probe shows that a 1D sinogram SSM cannot surpass the Radon projection of the 2D image flow prior itself ($+0.01$\,dB on 60 views, $-0.03$\,dB on $120^\circ$). Code and per-image metrics are released at \url{https://github.com/ZeroDevi1/flow-ct-guidance}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Deep generative priors offer a compelling alternative to task-specific supervised post-processing for ill-posed CT reconstruction: a single unconditional prior generalizes across acquisition geometries without retraining, while posterior sampling quantifies reconstruction uncertainty that deterministic networks hide~\cite{song2022scoremed,chung2022mcg,chung2023dps}. While early medical inverse solvers relied on score-based diffusion stochastic differential equations (SDEs), recent work has pivoted toward \emph{flow matching} and \emph{rectified flow}~\cite{lipman2023flow,liu2023rectified}, which learn a continuous velocity field transporting noise to data along nearly straight ODE trajectories.

Removing stochastic noise injection accelerates inference, but it fundamentally alters how measurement guidance behaves. Without per-step re-noising to absorb off-manifold perturbations, overly aggressive or poorly scaled updates can permanently derail the ODE trajectory. Although several recent papers apply flow priors to CT~\cite{evangelista2026pgfm,eFMCT2026,lucid2026} or adapt diffusion inverse solvers to flows~\cite{pokle2024otode,martin2025pnpflow,kim2025flowdps}, they vary the prior, architecture, dataset, and solver schedule simultaneously. As a result, practitioners lack a controlled, apples-to-apples comparison answering a basic question: \emph{given a fixed flow-matching CT prior, which measurement-injection rule should be used, and when does each rule fail?}

To answer this question cleanly, we fix the prior, integrator, dataset, tuning protocol, and evaluation suite, and vary only how measurements enter each Euler step. Specifically, we evaluate three canonical paradigms---gradient guidance (M1), pseudo-inverse guidance (M2), and proximal data consistency (M3)---alongside filtered back-projection (FBP) and a same-backbone supervised FBPConvNet~\cite{jin2017fbpconvnet}. Going beyond sparse-view and low-dose scans, we extend the controlled benchmark to severe limited-angle geometries ($120^\circ$ and $90^\circ$) and test a natural dual-domain hypothesis: whether completing unmeasured sinogram angles inside the ODE sampler with a lightweight 1D state-space model (SSM)~\cite{gu2024mamba} and Helgason--Ludwig consistency conditions (HLCC)~\cite{helgason1999radon} can strengthen proximal consistency.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Controlled multi-protocol benchmark.} We compare M1, M2, and M3 on a shared 19.8M-parameter rectified-flow prior across sparse-view (30/60/90), limited-angle ($120^\circ$/$90^\circ$), and low-dose ($I_0{=}10^5, 10^4$) CT on public TotalSegmentator data, using validation-only tuning and patient-level cluster bootstrap confidence intervals.
  \item \textbf{Scale-invariant guidance units.} We normalize DPS steps by $\Delta t / \sqrt{s_x}$ and proximal weights by $s_x$ (and $\bar{w}\,s_x$ for weighted full-angle operators), where $s_x{=}\operatorname{tr}(\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top\A_{\mathrm{lin}})/N^2$. This prevents NFE ablations and cross-geometry comparisons from silently rescaling guidance strength.
  \item \textbf{Regime-dependent insights and failure diagnosis.} We establish that M2 excels on single-sample moderate sparse-view CT, whereas M3 dominates limited-angle CT ($+2.1$ to $+2.6$\,dB), few-step sampling (20 NFE), and posterior-mean estimation ($+3.9$\,dB over FBPConvNet), and we diagnose why DPS-style unit-norm steps collapse on flow ODEs.
  \item \textbf{Negative-result probe of 1D sinogram completion inside a 2D flow sampler.} We show that a 2.57M-parameter 1D angle-sequence SSM cannot outperform the 2D image prior's own Radon projection $\A_{\mathrm{full}}(\hat\x_1)$ on unmeasured views, exposing a geometric representation bottleneck while confirming a modest, statistically significant HLCC benefit in limited-angle gaps.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Diffusion inverse solvers for CT.}
Posterior sampling with diffusion priors falls into three broad families: likelihood-gradient backpropagation through the denoiser (DPS~\cite{chung2023dps}, MCG~\cite{chung2022mcg}), Gaussian pseudo-inverse corrections ($\Pi$GDM~\cite{song2023pigdm}), and proximal or null-space projections alternating with denoising (DiffPIR~\cite{zhu2023diffpir}, DDS~\cite{chung2024dds}, DAPS~\cite{zhang2025daps}, RED-diff~\cite{mardani2024reddiff}). In CT, score-based priors have been tailored to sparse-view, 3D, and limited-angle reconstruction~\cite{song2022scoremed,chung2023diffusionmbir,song2024diffusionblend,liu2023dolce}, and DM4CT~\cite{shi2026dm4ct} recently benchmarked ten diffusion solvers. Our study is complementary: we hold a rectified-flow prior fixed and isolate the injection rule.

\paragraph{Flow matching for inverse problems and CT.}
General flow-based inverse solvers adapt diffusion guidance via optimal-transport paths (OT-ODE~\cite{pokle2024otode}), plug-and-play projections (PnP-Flow~\cite{martin2025pnpflow}), and flow-Tweedie decompositions (FlowDPS~\cite{kim2025flowdps}). For CT specifically, Evangelista~\cite{evangelista2026pgfm} compares flow solvers on Mayo LDCT, EFMCT~\cite{eFMCT2026} reuses adjacent velocity fields with data consistency, and LUCID~\cite{lucid2026} adapts flow trajectories to undersampling sparsity. Recent work also highlights the sensitivity of guidance schedules~\cite{moroy2026guidanceweights,barthakur2026leegs,bang2026trips}. Unlike these studies, we enforce view- and step-invariant parameterizations, strict validation-only tuning, and patient-clustered statistical testing across sparse-view, limited-angle, and noisy regimes.

\paragraph{Supervised and dual-domain CT reconstruction.}
Supervised post-processing networks such as FBPConvNet~\cite{jin2017fbpconvnet} and unrolled architectures such as LEARN~\cite{chen2018learn} deliver strong in-distribution accuracy but require per-protocol retraining. Dual-domain models and state-space architectures (e.g., MambaRoll~\cite{kabas2024mambaroll}, MambaMIR~\cite{huang2024mambamir}, RCL-Mamba~\cite{duan2026rclmamba}) process projection and image features jointly. Here we specifically test whether plugging a lightweight 1D angle-sequence SSM completer into a frozen 2D flow prior yields additive gains.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Forward Imaging Model}
Let $\x\in[-1,1]^{N\times N}$ ($N{=}256$) denote an axial CT slice normalized from the $[-1000,1000]$\,HU window, and let $u = m\odot(\x+1)/2 \in [0,1]^{N\times N}$ be the physical attenuation masked to the inscribed reconstruction disk $m$. For a set of projection angles $\Theta$, the parallel-beam Radon transform $R_\Theta$ is affine in $\x$:
\begin{equation}
  \A(\x) = R_\Theta(u) = \A_{\mathrm{lin}}\x + \bm{b},
  \qquad \A_{\mathrm{lin}}\x = R_\Theta(m\odot\x/2),
  \quad \bm{b} = R_\Theta(m/2).
\end{equation}
We implement $R_\Theta$ in pure PyTorch via bilinear rotation and summation (caching the rotation grids per operator), with exact adjoint $\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top$ computed via automatic differentiation (verified to $<10^{-4}$ relative adjoint error). We consider three degradation families on a 180-angle full grid ($1^\circ$ spacing in $[0,\pi)$):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Sparse-view:} $|\Theta|\in\{30,60,90\}$ equispaced angles in $[0,\pi)$, noiseless.
  \item \textbf{Limited-angle:} contiguous arcs $[0^\circ,120^\circ)$ ($120$ angles, $60^\circ$ missing wedge) and $[0^\circ,90^\circ)$ ($90$ angles, $90^\circ$ missing wedge), noiseless.
  \item \textbf{Low-dose:} full 180 views corrupted by Poisson photon noise $I\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(I_0 e^{-\mu y})$, $\y=-\log(I/I_0)/\mu$ with $\mu{=}0.06$, at $I_0{=}10^5$ (main) and $I_0{=}10^4$ (photon-starved). Noise is seeded deterministically per test slice so every method receives identical $\y$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Rectified-Flow Prior and Model Architectures}
We train a single unconditional velocity network $\vel(\x_t,t)$ on linear interpolants $\x_t=(1-t)\bm\epsilon+t\x$ ($\bm\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$) by minimizing $\mathbb E\|\vel(\x_t,t)-(\x-\bm\epsilon)\|_2^2$ with logit-normal time sampling~\cite{esser2024sd3}. At any state $(\x_t,t)$, the one-step clean-image and noise estimates are
\begin{equation}
  \hat\x_1 = \x_t + (1-t)\,\vel(\x_t,t),
  \qquad \hat{\bm\epsilon} = \x_t - t\,\vel(\x_t,t).
  \label{eq:tweedie}
\end{equation}
Unguided sampling integrates $\dot\x=\vel(\x_t,t)$ from $t{=}0$ to $1$ with uniform Euler step $\Delta t = 1/\mathrm{NFE}$.

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} illustrates both neural architectures used in this study alongside the four measurement-injection pathways, and Table~\ref{tab:arch} lists their exact layer specifications:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Shared 19.8M 2D U-Net backbone:} A five-stage ADM-style U-Net with base width 64, channel multipliers $(1,1,2,2,4)$ across resolutions $256^2 \to 16^2$, two residual blocks per encoder stage (three per decoder stage with skip concatenations), 4-head self-attention at $16^2$ and in the bottleneck, sinusoidal time embeddings projected to 256 dimensions for Adaptive GroupNorm FiLM modulation, and a zero-initialized $3{\times}3$ output convolution. The supervised FBPConvNet baseline reuses this \emph{exact} architecture with $t{\equiv}0$ and predicts a residual on top of FBP.
  \item \textbf{2.57M Bidirectional Angle-Sequence SSM (\texttt{dcfull} probe):} Takes the RMS-normalized 3-channel sinogram $[s_{\mathrm{pred}}, \y\odot m, m]\in\mathbb R^{3\times 180\times 256}$, extends it periodically to 360 rows via $s(\theta{+}\pi,r){=}s(\theta,-r)$, encodes each detector row with two 1D convolutions ($256\to 64$, 16 channels) plus a linear projection to $d{=}256$ with cyclic positional features ($\sin/\cos\theta, \sin/\cos 2\theta$), processes two circular laps ($L{=}720$) through 4 bidirectional selective SSM blocks ($d_i{=}512, d_{\mathrm{state}}{=}16$, conditioned on $t$ via FiLM and accelerated by a fused Triton scan kernel), and decodes per-angle residual $\delta s$ and log-variance $\log\sigma_\theta^2\in[-10,4]$ from the second lap.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Measurement-Injection Rules and Scale-Invariant Units}
To compare rules cleanly across view counts and NFE, we compute two operator energy scales once per geometry using 16 Gaussian probes: $s_x = \operatorname{tr}(\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top\A_{\mathrm{lin}})/N^2$ and $s_y = \operatorname{tr}(\A_{\mathrm{lin}}\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top)/M$, where $M$ is the measurement dimension. Let $\bm r = \y - \A(\hat\x_1)$ and $t_{+}=t+\Delta t$.

\paragraph{M1: Gradient guidance (DPS-style).}
For $t\ge t_s$, we subtract the gradient of the residual norm with step- and view-invariant scaling:
\begin{equation}
  \x_{t+\Delta t} = \x_t + \Delta t\,\Big(\vel - \frac{\zeta}{\sqrt{s_x}}\,\nabla_{\x_t}\|\A(\hat\x_1)-\y\|_2\Big).
\end{equation}
Multiplying by $\Delta t$ keeps the total integrated guidance invariant to NFE, while dividing by $\sqrt{s_x}$ aligns $\zeta$ across view counts. Because the denoiser Jacobian spikes near $t{=}0$, we also tune the start time $t_s$.

\paragraph{M2: Pseudo-inverse guidance ($\Pi$GDM-style).}
Approximating $p(\x_1\mid\x_t)\approx\mathcal N(\hat\x_1, r_t^2 I)$ with $r_t^2=(1-t)^2/(t^2+(1-t)^2)$, we solve in measurement space using $K{=}5$ conjugate-gradient (CG) iterations:
\begin{equation}
  \big(\A_{\mathrm{lin}}\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top + (\sigma_y^2/r_t^2 + \eta\,s_y)I\big)\bm z = \bm r,
  \qquad
  \x_{t+\Delta t} = \x_t + \Delta t\,\vel + \zeta\,t_{+}\,\Big(\tfrac{\partial\hat\x_1}{\partial\x_t}\Big)^{\!\top}\A_{\mathrm{lin}}^\top\bm z,
\end{equation}
with $\eta{=}10^{-3}$ and $\sigma_y{=}0$ (no explicit noise model in our controlled comparison).

\paragraph{M3: Proximal data consistency (DDS/DDNM-style).}
Avoiding backpropagation through the U-Net entirely, M3 solves a damped image-space proximal problem with $K{=}5$ CG iterations and recomposes the next ODE state:
\begin{equation}
  \hat\x_1^{\mathrm{dc}} = \arg\min_{\x}\|\A(\x)-\y\|_2^2 + \tilde\lambda s_x\|\x-\hat\x_1\|_2^2,
  \qquad
  \x_{t+\Delta t} = (1-t_{+})\hat{\bm\epsilon} + t_{+}\hat\x_1^{\mathrm{dc}}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{M3-Full (\texttt{dcfull}): In-sampler sinogram completion and HLCC probe.}
In sparse-view and limited-angle CT, M3 constrains only measured angles $\Theta_m \subset \Theta_{\mathrm{full}}$. To test whether filling missing angles inside the sampler helps, we extend M3 to the full 180-angle operator $\A_{\mathrm{full}}$ with per-angle confidence weights $W{=}\operatorname{diag}(w_\theta)$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat\x_1^{\mathrm{dc}} = \arg\min_{\x}\big\|W^{1/2}(\A_{\mathrm{full}}\x - \hat{\y})\big\|_2^2 + \tilde\lambda\,\bar{w}\,s_x^{\mathrm{full}}\|\x-\hat\x_1\|_2^2,
  \label{eq:dcfull}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{w}{=}\frac{1}{180}\sum_\theta w_\theta$ ensures exact continuity with M3 as unmeasured weights vanish ($w_{\theta\notin\Theta_m}{\to}0$). Measured rows are clamped to $\hat{\y}_\theta{=}\y_\theta$ with $w_\theta{=}1$. We compare three sources for unmeasured rows: (i) circular linear interpolation (\texttt{interp}) using the Radon periodicity $s(\theta{+}\pi,r){=}s(\theta,-r)$; (ii) the prior's own projection (\texttt{pred}, $\hat{\y}_\theta{=}[\A_{\mathrm{full}}(\hat\x_1)]_\theta$); and (iii) a trained 2.57M-parameter 4-layer bidirectional selective SSM (\texttt{ssm}) scanning the $2\pi$-periodic sinogram conditioned on $t$, predicting both residual $\delta s$ and log-variance $\log\sigma_\theta^2$ with normalized confidence weights $w_\theta{=}\min(1,\kappa\,\bar{\sigma}^2/\sigma_\theta^2)$. Optionally, we apply a ridge-regularized Helgason--Ludwig moment projection $\Pi_{\mathrm{HLCC}}(\hat{\y};w)$ (Legendre orders $0\ldots 6$) to unmeasured rows before Eq.~\eqref{eq:dcfull}.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Dataset and splits.}
We use TotalSegmentator v2.0.1~\cite{wasserthal2023totalseg} (1{,}228 CT volumes, 1.5\,mm isotropic, CC-BY-4.0) with its official patient-level split: 1{,}082 train, 57 validation, and 89 test patients. Up to 40 axial slices per volume ($>10\%$ body fraction) are uniformly sampled and center-cropped to $256^2$, yielding 43{,}220 train, 2{,}280 validation, and 3{,}560 test slices. All test evaluations share a fixed benchmark set \emph{test200} (20 test patients $\times$ 10 slices). All hyperparameters are tuned exclusively on 50 evenly spaced validation slices.

\paragraph{Models and training.}
The shared flow prior is a 19.8M-parameter U-Net (base width 64, channel multipliers $[1,1,2,2,4]$, self-attention at $16^2$), trained for 120k steps at batch size 32 ($\approx 89$ epochs; 6.3\,h on a single RTX~5090 with \texttt{torch.compile} and EMA 0.9995). For supervised comparison on sparse-view and low-dose protocols, we train a protocol-specific FBPConvNet with the identical U-Net backbone ($t{\equiv}0$) for 10k steps at batch size 16 ($\approx 3.7$ epochs; a conservative lower bound relative to the prior's training budget). For the \texttt{dcfull} probe, we freeze the prior and train the 2.57M-parameter bidirectional SSM completer for 15k steps at batch size 16 on mixed sparse-view (60\%) and limited-angle (40\%) masks with decoupled MSE + calibrated heteroscedastic NLL ($0.01\times$) + HLCC loss ($0.1\times$), accelerated $45\times$ via a fused Triton selective-scan kernel (36\,min on a single A100-40GB). Complete tuning grids and selected parameters are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:tuning}.

\paragraph{Metrics and statistical testing.}
We report PSNR (dynamic range 2 over $[-1000,1000]$\,HU) and SSIM strictly inside the reconstruction disk, perceptual distance LPIPS (AlexNet)~\cite{zhang2018lpips}, and relative data-consistency error $\mathrm{DC}=\|\A(\hat\x)-\y\|_2/\|\y\|_2$. Because test slices are grouped by patient, all 95\% confidence intervals and paired differences use patient-level cluster bootstrapping (2{,}000 resamples over the 20 test patients).

  \item \textbf{Moderate sparse-view (60 views): M2 leads.} M2 reaches 36.93\,dB, beating FBPConvNet by $+1.43$\,dB (CI $[0.95, 1.91]$) and M3 by $+0.97$\,dB (CI $[0.35, 1.55]$). Visually (Fig.~\ref{fig:qual}), M2 yields the sharpest anatomical boundaries, whereas FBPConvNet over-smooths fine structure and M3 preserves the grain of the clinical reference scan. At 30 views, M2 matches FBPConvNet ($-0.06$\,dB, CI $[-0.69, 0.58]$); at 90 views, M3 edges ahead (38.62\,dB, $+1.21$\,dB over FBPConvNet, CI $[0.18, 2.35]$).
  \item \textbf{Limited-angle ($120^\circ$ and $90^\circ$): M3 decisively dominates M2.} When an entire $60^\circ$ or $90^\circ$ wedge is missing (Fig.~\ref{fig:la}), M3 beats M2 by \textbf{$+2.12$\,dB} at $120^\circ$ (29.32 vs.\ 27.20\,dB, CI $[1.08, 3.26]$) and \textbf{$+2.63$\,dB} at $90^\circ$ (24.38 vs.\ 21.74\,dB, CI $[1.58, 3.71]$), while also cutting LPIPS by 32--36\% and achieving $3\text{--}4\times$ tighter data consistency. On severe missing wedges, the measurement-space Gram matrix $\A\A^\top$ becomes ill-conditioned; backpropagating its pseudo-inverse through the U-Net Jacobian destabilizes M2 (validation PSNR collapses from 25.87\,dB at $\zeta{=}0.5$ to 16.08\,dB at $\zeta{=}1.0$), whereas M3's damped image-space proximal step stays well-behaved.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Posterior-Mean Averaging Reverses the Sparse-View Ranking}
Averaging $K$ independent posterior samples from different initial noises dramatically benefits M3 more than M2 (Table~\ref{tab:mean}). At 60 views ($K{=}8$), M3 gains $+3.46$\,dB to reach \textbf{39.41\,dB}, overtaking M2 by $+0.82$\,dB (CI $[0.28, 1.43]$) and beating FBPConvNet by $+3.92$\,dB (CI $[2.97, 4.95]$). At 30 views ($K{=}4$), M3 similarly overtakes M2 by $+1.21$\,dB (CI $[0.43, 2.04]$).

The posterior standard deviation maps (Fig.~\ref{fig:unc}) explain why: M2's variance is small and concentrated tightly along anatomical edges, whereas M3's variance is spatially diffuse and noise-like across null-space components, which cancel constructively upon averaging.

\subsection{Why Uncalibrated Gradient Guidance (M1) Collapses on Flow ODEs}
Across 30, 60, and 90 views, M1 saturates at a nearly identical plateau of 24.40--24.54\,dB (mean per-image difference between 60 and 90 views is only $0.0003$\,dB), leaves a $10\times$ larger consistency error ($\mathrm{DC}\approx 3.8\%$), and hallucinates anatomical structures (Fig.~\ref{fig:qual}). Two coupled mechanisms cause this failure:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Early-time Jacobian spike.} A preliminary probe on 4 validation slices (40k-step checkpoint, 60 views) measured the M1 guidance gradient RMS at $\approx 1.9$ at $t{=}0$ versus $0.03\text{--}0.07$ for $t\ge 0.2$---a $\sim\!60\times$ drop. Without delaying guidance ($t_s{=}0$), validation PSNR never exceeds 13.8\,dB.
  \item \textbf{Non-vanishing unit-norm steps.} Differentiating $\|\A(\hat\x_1)-\y\|_2$ normalizes out $\|\bm r\|_2$, so the correction magnitude fails to shrink as consistency improves. Combined with a constant $\zeta$ and deterministic ODE transport, this traps samples on a view-independent plateau. Time-dependent schedules~\cite{moroy2026guidanceweights} are required to rehabilitate gradient guidance on flows.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Photon Noise Exposes the Limit of Noise-Free Consistency}
Under Poisson noise at $I_0{=}10^5$ (Table~\ref{tab:main}), supervised FBPConvNet outperforms all noise-unaware flow rules (35.32\,dB vs.\ 33.16\,dB for M2 and 30.60\,dB for M3; FBPConvNet$-$M2 $=+2.17$\,dB, CI $[1.86, 2.49]$). M3 requires a $100\times$ stronger proximal weight ($\tilde\lambda{=}30$) to avoid fitting measurement noise directly into the image. When strengths tuned at $I_0{=}10^5$ are transferred without re-tuning to photon-starved $I_0{=}10^4$, the ranking inverts completely: FBP 18.03, M3 20.63, M2 22.53, and M1 24.50\,dB---M1 survives best precisely because it enforces the noisy measurements least. Closing this gap requires an explicit Poisson likelihood or noise-calibrated $\sigma_y{>}0$.

Three clear conclusions emerge from this probe:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Zero-gain stop-loss verdict.} Against M3, \texttt{dcfull-ssm + HLCC} yields $+0.007$\,dB on 60 views (CI $[-0.056, +0.077]$) and $-0.028$\,dB on $120^\circ$ (CI $[-0.060, -0.003]$), triggering our pre-registered 0.3\,dB stop-loss criterion. In every setting, validation tuning pushed $\kappa$ to its lower grid boundary ($0.05$), effectively turning off unmeasured-row weighting.
  \item \textbf{Geometric root cause.} The validation sinogram error column explains why: even after 15k steps, the 2.57M-parameter 1D sinogram SSM never surpasses the 19.8M-parameter 2D image prior's own Radon projection $\A_{\mathrm{full}}(\hat\x_1)$ (0.00395 vs.\ 0.00372 on 60 views; 0.00661 vs.\ 0.00644 on $120^\circ$; 0.00677 vs.\ 0.00660 on $90^\circ$). Because a point $(x,y)$ traces a curved sinusoid $r{=}x\cos\theta{+}y\sin\theta$ across detector bins, a 1D row-tokenized SSM lacks exact 2D backprojection geometry and cannot refine a projection that already lies strictly in $\operatorname{range}(\A)$ from a much stronger 2D anatomical prior.
  \item \textbf{Targeted HLCC confirmation.} On $120^\circ$ limited-angle CT, adding HLCC projection to \texttt{dcfull-ssm} yields a small but statistically significant recovery of $+0.065$\,dB (CI $[+0.041, +0.091]$), confirming that moment consistency removes physically inconsistent completion errors in contiguous missing wedges---even though it cannot overcome an inferior completer.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Implications for future method design.}
Our negative result on in-sampler 1D sinogram completion points to where genuine gains above M3 and M2 must come from: (i) \emph{conditioning or fine-tuning the 2D flow prior itself} (e.g., LoRA/ControlNet conditioning on FBP or measurement features, or conditional flow matching directly from FBP to clean CT), which preserves full 2D spatial geometry; (ii) \emph{noise-aware likelihood updates} (weighted least squares with Poisson variance estimates) to close the low-dose gap; and (iii) \emph{hybrid early-M3 / late-M2 or learned time-dependent guidance schedules}~\cite{moroy2026guidanceweights,barthakur2026leegs} that combine M3's stability with M2's sharpness.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our forward operator is a simulated 2D parallel-beam projector applied to reconstructed clinical volumes; fan-/cone-beam geometry, real raw projection data, operator mismatch, and 3D inter-slice consistency~\cite{chung2023diffusionmbir,song2024diffusionblend} remain outside this scope. Clinical reference scans contain native scanner noise, capping attainable PSNR. FBPConvNet was trained for 10k steps ($\approx 3.7$ epochs) versus 120k steps ($\approx 89$ epochs) for the flow prior, so its scores represent a lower bound; supervised limited-angle baselines are deferred to future work. Finally, M1's gradient-norm probe used a 40k-step intermediate checkpoint, and posterior-mean experiments evaluated $K{=}8$ (60 views) and $K{=}4$ (30 views).
